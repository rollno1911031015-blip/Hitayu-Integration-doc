A stock audit is the process of comparing what your system says you have against what is actually sitting on the warehouse shelf. Discrepancies creep in over time due to shipping errors, damaged goods that weren't recorded, counting mistakes in inward stock, or theft. Running regular audits ensures your inventory data stays trustworthy and your customer-facing availability figures remain accurate.

---

## When to Run a Stock Audit

Consider running a stock audit in any of these situations:

- **Scheduled cycle count** — a routine periodic check (weekly, monthly, or quarterly) covering a subset of your SKUs
- **Full physical inventory** — an end-of-period count of all products, often aligned with a financial year-end
- **Post-incident investigation** — when a significant discrepancy is noticed in orders or returns
- **After warehouse changes** — following a warehouse move, reorganisation, or handover to new staff

<Tip>
  Best practice is to run audits during **off-peak hours** — overnight or on weekends — when order activity is minimal. This reduces the risk of stock movements occurring mid-count that could distort your comparison figures.
</Tip>

---

## Navigating to Stock Audits

Go to **Admin → Inventory → Stock Audit** (`/admin/inventory/stock-audit`). This segment lists all past and in-progress audits for your account.

---

## Creating a New Audit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start a new audit">
    On the Stock Audit page, click **New Stock Audit**. A creation form will appear.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the warehouse">
    Choose the **warehouse** you are auditing. Each audit is scoped to a single warehouse so you can run audits for multiple locations independently.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the audit">
    Give the audit a recognisable name — for example, *"Monthly Cycle Count – Delhi Warehouse – June 2025"*. This makes it easy to find in the audit history later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the audit">
    Click **Create Audit**. The audit is created in **Draft** status and you can now begin adding items to count.
  </Step>
</Steps>

---

## Adding Items to an Audit

Once an audit has been created, you need to specify which product variants you are counting and record the physical quantity found on the shelf.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the audit">
    Click on the audit name in the Stock Audit list to open its detail view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add product variants">
    Click **Add Item**. Search for the product variant you have physically counted and select it. The system will display the **expected quantity** — this is the quantity Hitayu ERP currently holds for that variant in the selected warehouse.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the physical count">
    In the **Physical Count** field, enter the quantity you actually counted on the shelf. This is your ground-truth figure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record count timestamp">
    The system records a `counted_at` timestamp for each item automatically when you save the entry. This gives you a precise audit trail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat for all items">
    Continue adding all product variants in scope for this audit. You can save progress and return to add more items before completing the audit.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Understanding Expected vs. Physical Count

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| **Expected Quantity** | The system's current warehouse stock figure at the time the item was added to the audit |
| **Physical Count** | The actual quantity your team counted on the physical shelf |
| **Discrepancy** | The difference between expected and physical (positive = surplus, negative = shortage) |

<Note>
  The expected quantity is captured at the moment you add each item to the audit. If stock movements occur after that point (e.g. new MRNs or order dispatches), they will not retroactively change the expected figure in the audit.
</Note>

## Completing the Audit

When all items have been counted and entered, you are ready to finalise the audit and apply the corrections to your live stock levels.

<Warning>
  **Completing an audit is irreversible and will overwrite your current system stock levels.** Every product variant included in the audit will have its warehouse quantity set to the physical count you recorded, regardless of what the system previously held. Do not complete an audit if the count data is incomplete or if counting is still in progress.
</Warning>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the discrepancy summary">
    Before completing, scroll through the audit items and review the **Discrepancy** column. Investigate any unexpectedly large variances — they may indicate a counting error rather than a genuine stock difference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Complete Audit">
    When you are confident the counts are accurate, click **Complete Audit** at the top of the audit detail page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the action">
    A confirmation dialog will appear summarising the number of items and warehouses affected. Click **Confirm** to proceed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="System updates stock levels">
    Hitayu ERP will:
    - Set the warehouse stock quantity for each audited variant to the recorded physical count
    - Create **Adjustment** entries in the Stock Ledger for every item with a discrepancy, referencing this audit
    - Mark the audit as **Completed** and record the `completed_at` timestamp and the user who completed it
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Product variants that were **not** included in the audit are unaffected. Only the items explicitly added to the audit will have their quantities updated.
</Note>

## Viewing Audit History and Discrepancies

All completed audits are preserved in full detail. On the **Admin → Inventory → Stock Audit** page, each completed audit row shows:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| **Audit Name** | The name given when the audit was created |
| **Warehouse** | The warehouse that was audited |
| **Items Counted** | Total number of product variants included |
| **Discrepancies** | Count of items where physical count differed from expected |
| **Completed By** | The user who finalised the audit |
| **Completed At** | Date and time the audit was closed |

Click on any completed audit to view the full item-by-item breakdown, including the expected quantity, physical count, and the resulting adjustment for every SKU.

<Tip>
  Cross-reference the audit completion entries in the **Stock Ledger** (under **Admin → Inventory → Stock Ledger**, filtering by movement type **Adjustment**) to see the precise quantity change applied to each variant as a result of the audit.
</Tip>

## Frequently Asked Questions

<Accordion title="Can I edit an audit after it is completed?">
  No. Completed audits are locked to preserve the integrity of your stock history. If you discover an error after completion, raise a manual stock adjustment through a new audit covering just the affected items, or contact your system administrator.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens to stock reservations during an audit?">
  Active stock reservations (held against open orders) are not cancelled by an audit. After completion, the available quantity shown in the current-stock view will reflect the new audited quantity minus any active reservations. If the audited count was lower than the reserved quantity, you may see a negative available stock — you should review and resolve those open orders promptly.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I audit multiple warehouses at once?">
  Each audit is scoped to a single warehouse. To audit multiple warehouses simultaneously, create a separate audit for each location. They can run in parallel — completing one does not affect the others.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who can create and complete stock audits?">
  Users require the **Inventory: Stock Audit** permission (plus **Inventory: Manage**) to create, edit, and complete audits. Contact your administrator to request access if needed.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Does completing an audit affect the Stock Ledger?">
  Yes. For every item where the physical count differs from the expected quantity, Hitayu ERP creates an **Adjustment** entry in the Stock Ledger. These entries are tagged with the source `stock_audit` and link back to the audit record so you have a full traceability chain.
</Accordion>
